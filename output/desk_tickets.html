Reflection

My evaluation of the agent team after the full run (Problem 9).

1. How I'd evaluate the team on each ticket

Ticket 101 (Tauhid's tee): The team made the right business calls but was inefficient. On run 1, the Chairman sent the ticket to Stock Desk first, and Stock Desk found 0 Classic Bulldog Tees in size S and saw that Bulldog Print Co was blocked by invoice 501 ($840, 3 days overdue). Instead of forcing a purchase order, it went to Treasury, which put up an $840 slip, and Client Relations drafted an honest note to Tauhid with no promised date. That's exactly how I'd want an analyst to think: fix the blocker first. The weak part is that it took 3 runs. Run 1 hit the 60,000-token cap, and run 3 hit the 30-request cap. The Chairman also wandered into ticket 102's rent inside 101's run, and Stock Desk ordered the same $8 tee twice, because it could only see pending requests, not ones already paid. I approved both, so $8 of my cash went to a tee nobody needed. In the end I closed 101 myself. Grade: good judgment, poor memory and discipline.

Ticket 102 (rent): The rent ($2,400) was already paid during 101's run, so when 102 ran on its own, the team found something odd. Real Estate called get_lease_status and saw the next due date was now 2026-10-02 ("not_due", 32 days away), and flagged that the landlord's email ("due in 2 days") conflicted with the lease record. Treasury reported $144 in checking and said it couldn't see the rent amount with its own tools. That's least privilege working: it doesn't have the lease tool. The Chairman then had Client Relations draft a clarification to Elm City Properties instead of paying again. The good news is no double rent payment, and the agents trusted the database over an email. The bad news is that nobody realized the "conflict" was our own payment #4. They can't see payment history, so a real landlord would have gotten a confusing note. I closed 102 myself.

Ticket 103 (Yale AI Club hoodies): On run 1, the Chairman called Stock Desk and Treasury at the same time. Stock Desk found 8 Basic Hoodies in size M against the 20 asked (short 12) and saw that Bulldog Print Co was no longer blocked, because invoice 501 was already paid. It tried a purchase order for the 12 missing hoodies ($264), and the tool refused it because checking only had $144. Treasury put up slip #5, a price override of $1,044 total for 20 hoodies (10% off the $58 list, $52.20 each, above the $22 cost), and I approved it. On run 2, Treasury couldn't see that slip #5 was already approved, so it put up slip #6 for the same $52.20 on just the 8 in stock ($417.60), and I approved that too. Client Relations drafted notes to the club (drafts #5 and #6) offering the 8 at the pending price, with no date for the other 12. After 2 runs the Chairman still had it at awaiting approval, so I closed 103 myself. My take: The discount respected my 10% cap and stayed well above the $22 floor, and the cash rule stopped a restock I couldn't afford. But it priced all 20 hoodies when only 8 were in stock, and then discounted the same 8 again because it couldn't see its own approved slip.

2. Actual vs. my expected plan

  • 101: I expected one clean run: Chairman → Stock Desk → Treasury ($840) → $8 PO → Client Relations. The call order matched on run 1, but it took 3 runs, pulled in Real Estate (not in my plan), and produced a duplicate $8 PO.
  • 102: I expected Real Estate → Treasury → one $2,400 slip. The payment happened, but earlier, inside 101's run. The 102 run itself made no slip, called Real Estate and Treasury at the same time, and added a landlord draft I didn't plan. Stock Desk stayed out, as expected.
  • 103: I expected 8 hoodies now, a discount of 10% or less on those 8, no PO because the $264 restock didn't fit, and a draft to the club. The 10% price ($52.20) and the club drafts happened, and there was no PO, but because the tool refused it for lack of cash, not because the team decided to skip it. The discount first went on all 20 hoodies (slip #5, $1,044), then a second run added an overlapping one on the 8 (slip #6, $417.60). It took 2 runs, pulled in Real Estate on run 1, and I closed it myself.
  • Cash: My plan ended at $152. The Cash tab shows $144. The $8 gap is the duplicate tee (payment #3), and that's the only difference: the two price overrides moved no cash, and the $264 hoodie restock was refused.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Ticket 102 is the clearest case. It needs one lookup (get_lease_status), one cash check, and one payment request, so a single agent could do it in about three tool calls. My team used the Chairman, Real Estate, Treasury and Client Relations, and Treasury couldn't even see the rent amount. A single agent would also have avoided my two biggest problems, the duplicate $8 tee and the Chairman paying rent inside 101's run, because both came from agents not sharing what the others had already done. The multi-agent setup earns its keep on 103, where stock, a blocked vendor, margins and limited cash all collide, and on safety, since each desk only holds the tools for its job.

4. Three new problems this team COULD solve with my tools

  • A customer wants 2 Crest Mugs (CC-MUG-CREST, 0 in stock). Stock Desk uses check_stock and list_vendors and finds Elm City Gifts (mugs, 3-day lead, no open invoice, so not blocked). It requests a PO at $3.50 each ($7), and Client Relations drafts the reply.
  • A student group wants 5 Yale Caps (CC-HAT-BLUE, 40 in stock) at a discount. Treasury uses get_sku_pricing (cost $6, list $22) and request_price_override above the $6 floor. Client Relations drafts the offer. No cash moves.
  • October rent notice (lease 1, now due 2026-10-02). Real Estate uses get_lease_status, and Treasury tries request_payment for $2,400. The tool refuses it because checking only has $144, so the Chairman escalates to me. That's the no-negative-balance rule doing its job.

5. Three new problems this team could NOT solve with my tools

  • Bringing money in. If Tauhid pays $28 for his tee or the AI Club pays for hoodies, there's no tool to record a sale. Cash only goes down, and at $144 the shop can't cover October's $2,400 rent. The agents can't fix cash flow.
  • Receiving stock and seeing history. When the tee PO arrives, no tool adds it to inventory, so size S stays at 0 forever. And no agent can see past payments. That's exactly why I got a duplicate $8 PO on 101 and a confused landlord draft on 102.
  • Actually talking to people. Asking Elm City Properties for a late-rent extension, or negotiating a payment plan with Bulldog Print Co on invoice 501, needs real conversations. My team only saves drafts (by design, there's no send tool), so a human has to do it.
